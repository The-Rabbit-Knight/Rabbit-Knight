### Building a Budget Gaming PC: A Component Breakdown

I’m excited to announce that I’m building a second PC, this time specifically for gaming! Researching parts and ensuring compatibility has been a rewarding challenge, and I’d love to share the process with you.

This build prioritizes value and performance for a smooth gaming experience without targeting the most demanding titles. It’s designed for entertainment, not professional esports.

#### CPU: AMD Ryzen 9 5900X

The centerpiece of this build is the powerful AMD Ryzen 9 5900X CPU. Its impressive PassMark score (39167/3470) suggests excellent multi-core and single-core performance. It’s still based on the old and reliable AM4 socket so I could skim a few bucks from motherboard price. It’s still a highly efficient piece of silicon utilizing all AM4 capabilities.

Key considerations:

PCIe 4.0 x16 support offers high-speed data transfer (16 GT/s) for any videocard within my price range ensuring CPU will never be a bottleneck. CPU supports up to 128 GB of DDR4 with solid wiggle room for over-clocking if required. While DDR5 offers performance benefits, DDR4 remains a cost-effective choice for this build. This CPU churns out a maximum of 105 watts of TDP. One needs to be cautious with our cooler!

#### Graphics Card: MSI GeForce VENTUS 2X Black RTX 4060 8GB

For smooth visuals I opted for the sleek MSI GeForce VENTUS 2X Black RTX 4060 8GB graphics card. This card boasts 8GB of GDDR6 memory and a PassMark score of 19569, translating to excellent 1080p and 1440p performance with impressive power efficiency (115W Max TDP). Technology like DLSS 2 and DLSS 3 has almost universal compatibility with game titles and further enhances frame rates in many games. The videocard supports the full bandwidth of PCIe 4.0 over all 16 PCI lanes available on the CPU.

Important note: The card’s 120mm height is a crucial factor when selecting a case and cooling solution.

#### Motherboard: ASRock B550M HDV

This motherboard offers essential features for a great price, including AM4 socket compatibility, B550 chipset support, and dual-channel memory. It supports just a single PCIe 4.0 x16 slot and “just” 64GB of RAM. However, AMD B550 chipset onboard ensures that the motherboard allows all features of Zen 3 platform to shine and enables full speed transmission rate between the chosen CPU and the Video Adapter. That is exactly what I want from my motherboard.

Motherboard Breakdown:

The B550 chipset delivers a cost-effective option compared to the X570 while offering an upgrade over the B450. Designed for Zen 3 Ryzen 5000 series CPUs with the AM4 socket. Provides PCIe 4.0 connectivity via the CPU for the primary PCIe x16 slot and 4 lanes for M.2 slot. Offers lower power consumption compared to B450 motherboards.

#### Cooling: Arctic Freezer 34 CPU Cooler

Let me be straight — I just want a PWM cooler for AM4 socket graded for 150W TDP. Arctic Freezer 34 CPU Cooler fits the bill perfectly! Now, that’s not a small device with a height of 157mm. Be careful with the case pick.

#### Power Supply: be quiet! Pure Power 11 400W

Be quiet! Pure Power 11 400W PSU is a reliable choice. This unit comfortably handles all the chosen components.

#### The Finishing Touches

I’m still finalizing the monitor selection, leaning toward an LG UltraGear model for its smooth refresh rate and vibrant display. In terms of memory and storage, I’ll be utilizing 2 modules of DDR4 3200MHz Corsair Vengeance RGB RS memory and a Silicon Power A80 TLC M.2 SSD to ensure smooth multitasking and fast loading times.
